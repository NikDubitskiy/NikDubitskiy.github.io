Research

How do a substrate and the local defect environment shape transport in a spin–orbit-coupled oxide?

My recent research follows the relationship between structure, local chemistry and transport in epitaxial SrIrO₃. The master’s thesis compares films grown by RF magnetron sputtering on five substrates: SrTiO₃, NdGaO₃, LSAT, LaAlO₃ and PMN–PT.

01

Epitaxial growth & structure

A series of approximately 35 nm films spans compressive and tensile lattice mismatch. X-ray diffraction identifies the oriented orthorhombic perovskite phase; AFM reveals how surface morphology changes between substrates.

02

Local chemistry & spin–orbit coupling

XPS probes the Ir chemical state, the Ir 4f spin–orbit splitting and the near-surface oxygen environment. Structural and spectroscopic observations help interpret substrate-dependent transport.

03

Charge transport & Hall effect

Resistance measurements from 4.2 to 300 K are combined with Hall measurements at 2 and 10 K in fields up to ±7 T. The Hall sign differs between substrates, consistent with changing electron and hole contributions in a multiband semimetal.

04

Electrical strain control

In SrIrO₃/PMN–PT, an applied voltage up to 300 V produces a room-temperature resistance change of approximately 0.022%. The response is consistent with a contribution from piezoelectric strain transfer.
